## ABSTRACT 

Learning from experience is essential for building the next generation of agents that continually expand their capabilities. Pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) already internalize rich world knowledge, yet this knowledge is rarely leveraged to drive policy improvement through online interaction. Realizing this potential efficiently remains challenging, as policy updates shift the agent’s interaction distri-bution, requiring the world model to adapt accordingly, while continually updating large pretrained models is computationally expensive. To address this challenge, we introduce D WELL , a model-based reinforcement learning (RL) framework that reframes a VLM as a recurrent latent transition operator, endowing it with the ability to predict, adjust, and act in latent space. Leveraging the VLM’s own representations and its dual role as both world model and policy, the agent con-tinually learns from real interactions to anticipate and evaluate action outcomes, and from latent imagination to improve its behavior. Across five interactive visual environments, D WELL achieves the strongest overall performance among train-able methods while substantially reducing the compute required for imagination, showing that effective world models for VLM agents need not reconstruct the future—they need to predict futures that are useful for action. Our code is publicly available at https://github.com/Tom111-lhx/Dwell .

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

AI agents (OpenAI, 2026; Team et al., 2026) have acquired broad capabilities through large-scale pretraining on human-generated data (Brown et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) and post-training with human demonstrations and preference feedback (Ziegler et al., 2019; Ouyang et al., 2022). Extending this progress beyond the limits of human-provided data (Villalobos et al., 2024) motivates agents that continually learn from experience generated through their own interactions with the environment (Silver & Sutton, 2025). Recent work (Wang et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026) has taken steps in this direction by using reinforcement learning (RL) to improve agents’ multi-step decision-making with environmental feedback. However, the substantial environment interaction often required by model-free approaches can make experience collection a bottleneck to scaling (Janner et al., 2019; Chandra et al., 2025). This raises a central question: how can agents achieve greater policy improvement from limited real experience? 

World models offer a way to learn more from real experience by predicting action outcomes and generating imagined trajectories for policy learning. For vision-language model (VLM) agents, models that synthesize observations have already served as training environments across embodied and web tasks (Zhu et al., 2026; Fang et al., 2025). In contrast, imagination in a compact latent space avoids reconstructing observations at every predicted step (Hafner et al., 2019; 2023), while predictive representation learning can emphasize predictable structure over incidental visual detail (Assran et al., 2023; Maes et al., 2026). Pretrained VLMs already encode rich world knowledge, suggesting that their own representations could support latent imagination for policy learning. 1arXiv:2610.36925v1 [cs.AI] 27 Oct 2026 Accurate prediction alone does not guarantee compatibility: a world model’s latent states may not be readily usable by a VLM policy, even when they faithfully capture action outcomes (Assran et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026). A further difficulty arises as policy updates shift the agent’s interaction distribution toward states and actions poorly covered by the world model’s training data (Janner et al., 2019). In these regions, errors in predicted transitions and rewards can bias subsequent policy updates, so gains in imagination may not translate into better behavior in the environment (Liu et al., 2026). Adapting to this evolving distribution can also be computationally expensive when it requires repeated updates to a large pretrained VLM. Together, these challenges motivate a shared representational basis for prediction and decision-making, coupled with efficient, continual correction of transition and reward predictions from real interaction. We introduce D WELL , a model-based RL framework that enables VLMs to predict, adjust, and act in their own latent space. By recasting the VLM as a recurrent latent transition operator, we equip it to reason about the state of the world from interaction history and improve its behavior through latent imagination. Following the joint-embedding principle (Assran et al., 2023), our method draws on the VLM’s rich world knowledge to actively infer a state representation in an abstract and compact space, rather than focusing on every detail of the observation. Exploiting the VLM’s dual role as a world model and policy, we instantiate both components using separate lightweight LoRA adapters (Hu et al., 2022) on the same VLM backbone, allowing independent, parameter-efficient adaptation while sharing a common representation interface. Together, recurrence and duality provide a unified framework for modeling and acting in dynamic environments through online learning. We conduct comprehensive experiments across five interactive visual environments spanning spatial planning, embodied navigation, manipulation, and long-horizon household tasks. under the reported training protocol, D WELL achieves the superior performance among trainable methods, while sub-stantially reducing the computational cost of imagination relative to a world-model baseline that reconstructs observations. In summary, the main contributions of the paper are as follows: • Learning and acting in a shared latent world. We recast a pretrained VLM as a recurrent latent transition operator, unifying world modeling and policy learning through a common belief interface. • Online policy improvement through latent imagination. We develop a learning loop in which real interactions continually refine the world and reward models, while imagined trajectories guide policy improvement. • Empirical gains in performance and efficiency. Our proposed method achieves strong overall performance among the evaluated trainable baselines under the reported training settings, while reducing imagination cost relative to a world model that synthesizes observations. 

## 2 METHOD 

A self-evolving agent should anticipate the consequences of its actions and use feedback from interaction to continually refine its predictions and behavior. Although VLMs have acquired rich world knowledge, they tend to be brittle when exposed to dynamic environments or when learning from experience. Our method, D WELL , anchors a VLM as a recurrent latent transition operator, morphing a static perceiver into an active predictor. Given streams of observations and actions from online interaction, it infers the most explainable states in the VLM’s own latent space, which can in turn be used for model-based planning in the absence of feedback. This cycle of interaction, refinement, and improvement enables the agent to continuously expand its understanding of the environment while adapting its actions accordingly.

VLM as World Model and Policy. We instantiate the world model and policy using separate LoRA adapters (Hu et al., 2022) on a shared, frozen pretrained VLM backbone. This separation supports independent, parameter-efficient adaptation of prediction and decision-making while keeping the pretrained backbone fixed. The latent slots retain their ordering across updates, providing a common interface for transition prediction, reward evaluation, and action selection. The policy πθ conditions on posterior states during real interaction and on predicted states during latent imagination. Thus, predicted states can be passed directly to the next state transition and decision without decoding them into the raw observation and re-encoding them. Learning a world model improves sample efficiency, enables planning and foresight, and enhances adaptability to environmental changes. The central questions are: (1) what state representation a good world model requires; and (2) how to make reliable predictions into the future to guide action selection. When faced with complex or high-dimensional observations, a naive approach is to optimize for reconstruction fidelity in the original space. However, this can be problematic, as it focuses too much on information that is redundant or irrelevant to solving the task, while also being computationally inefficient. VLMs can internalize rich knowledge in their latent space, providing us with a good starting point to build upon. We can leverage this internalized knowledge by operating in a compact latent space while attending only to the information most relevant to decision-making.

Role of the World Model Objectives. As shown in Figure 4, removing either predictive dynamics learning or representation grounding degrades performance on both S OKOBAN and N AVIGATION .The degradation is substantially larger without Ldyn , indicating that direct supervision of action-conditioned future states is essential for producing useful imagined trajectories. Effect of Online World Model Adaptation. We further freeze the world model after its initial fitting and continue policy optimization with the resulting fixed dynamics. This behavior is consistent with a policy-induced distribution shift: as the policy changes, it visits state–action regions that are increasingly different from those used to fit the initial model. Continually refreshing the world model with newly collected experience therefore keeps imagined trajectories aligned with the evolving policy distribution and enables sustained policy improvement. 

## 4 RELATED WORK 

Latent World Models for Behavior Learning. Model-based RL has long used learned dynamics to augment real experience with prediction and planning. Dreamer learns policies from trajectories imagined in a compact latent state space, while TD-MPC and its successors optimize behavior with decoder-free latent world models (Hafner et al., 2019; 2023; Hansen et al., 2024). More recent predictive-representation approaches replace observation reconstruction with latent prediction, enabling action-conditioned planning or policy learning directly in representation space (Assran et al., 2025; Bagatella et al., 2026). These methods establish that control need not operate on reconstructed observations. Our focus is complementary: we study how such a latent learning interface can be built into a VLM agent, so that the representations used for real decisions also form the state space of imagined policy learning. 

World Models for Agent Learning. Generative world models have recently become practical training environments for large-model agents. In embodied settings, video world models synthesize action-conditioned futures for VLA policy optimization (Zhu et al., 2026; Jia et al., 2026), while learned web simulators generate interaction experience for self-improvement in browser agents (Fang et al., 2025; Ding et al., 2026). A common design is to adapt the simulator to policy behavior and then hold it fixed while optimizing the policy. This separation makes model-generated experience reusable, but the simulator cannot incorporate new evidence arising during the subsequent policy updates. World-VLA-Loop (Liu et al., 2026) takes a step toward closing this loop by iteratively 8adding new policy rollouts to world-model training; however, policy learning still proceeds through autoregressively generated visual observations and inherits their long-horizon drift. D WELL instead uses the VLM’s predicted beliefs themselves as the interaction states: policy learning requires neither future-image generation nor re-encoding, and the latent world is refreshed from newly collected real experience throughout training. 

World Modeling within VLM Agents. A parallel line of work develops world-modeling capabili-ties inside the agent itself. VLM representations can provide semantically informed state features for control, and explicit state estimation and transition reasoning can improve multi-turn visual decision-making (Chen et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2026). Other approaches incorporate future prediction more directly into action learning: VLA-JEPA uses latent state prediction to learn action-relevant represen-tations, and unified world–action models jointly predict observations and actions (Sun et al., 2026; Cen et al., 2025). In these methods, world modeling primarily shapes the representation, reasoning process, or action predictor. D WELL gives the predicted state a different operational role: it is the state on which the policy continues to act and learn. A single frozen VLM backbone therefore supports two trainable roles through separate adapters: one learns action-conditioned latent dynamics, while the other learns the policy, and real interaction continually supervises both the predicted dynamics and their task outcomes. 

## 5 LIMITATION AND DISCUSSION 

When Does Latent Imagination Help? The benefit of latent imagination is not uniform across the evaluated tasks. While D WELL shows clear gains on S OKOBAN , F ROZEN LAKE , and EB-ALFRED, its advantage is smaller on N AVIGATION and P RIMITIVE SKILL . This pattern is consistent with the hypothesis that imagination is most useful when predicted future states provide information beyond what is already available from the current observation, whereas its benefit may diminish when action selection is largely observation-driven or depends on dynamics that are difficult to capture compactly. A natural next step is therefore to make imagination selective, allowing the agent to decide when and how far to imagine rather than applying a fixed rollout horizon uniformly across states and tasks. 

What Must a Latent World Preserve? Operating directly in a compact latent space avoids the cost of reconstructing future observations, but introduces a fundamental trade-off between abstraction and decision sufficiency. Representations that discard visually incidental details may also remove fine-grained dynamics that later become relevant for control, particularly in contact-rich interaction. Moreover, errors in latent transitions and reward prediction can accumulate during planning, making long-horizon rollouts increasingly unreliable. Our current deterministic latent dynamics do not explicitly represent such uncertainty, leaving the policy unable to distinguish a confident prediction from an ambiguous one. These limitations suggest that stronger latent world models may need to learn not only what is predictable, but what must be preserved for downstream decisions, together with uncertainty estimates that determine how far imagined experience should be trusted. 

Scope and Scalability. Our current evidence is limited to a single VLM backbone and five simulated interactive visual environments, and therefore does not yet establish how the same design scales with model capacity, richer action spaces, or real-world interaction. In addition, although the latent world is represented within the VLM, its representation is grounded using a frozen pretrained visual encoder; whether equally useful predictive structure can emerge solely from interaction remains an open question. Latent imagination is also not computationally free: while D WELL substantially reduces end-to-end GPU-hours relative to a generative world model, simpler latent dynamics remain cheaper, revealing a performance–compute trade-off rather than an absolute efficiency advantage. Extending this framework to larger VLMs, transferable world representations, and persistent learning across tasks and environments would be important steps toward agents that continually refine a shared model of their experience. 

## 6 CONCLUSION 

We presented D WELL , a model-based RL framework that enables VLM agents to learn from experi-ence in their own latent worlds. Rather than treating the world model as a separate simulator that 9reconstructs future observations, D WELL recasts the VLM as a recurrent latent transition operator and uses a shared representational interface for prediction, value estimation, and action. By interleaving real interaction with latent imagination, the agent can reuse limited environmental experience to improve its policy without generating future observations explicitly. Across diverse interactive visual environments, D WELL achieves strong overall performance under controlled interaction budgets while substantially reducing the computational cost of imagination relative to generative world models. Taken together, these results suggest that a useful world model need not reproduce the world itself; it needs to predict a future that the agent can directly act and learn upon.

A central design choice in D WELL is that the world model and policy operate on the same latent state interface: predicted states can therefore be consumed directly by the policy without an additional representation conversion. All other components, including the VLM backbone, world-model objectives, policy optimization, imagination horizon, and online training schedule, are kept unchanged. As shown in Table 2, separating the prediction and decision spaces consistently degrades performance across all three evaluated environments. These results provide evidence that the benefit of D WELL does not arise solely from learning latent dynamics: making imagined states directly compatible with the policy’s decision representation also facilitates learning from imagined experience.
