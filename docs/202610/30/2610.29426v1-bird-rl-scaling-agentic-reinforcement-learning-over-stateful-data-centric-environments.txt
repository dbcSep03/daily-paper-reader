Abstract 

Modern data-centric applications increasingly require language agents capable of operating over live, stateful database environments, where successful problem resolution depends not merely on initial SQL generation, but on reasoning about evolving database states, interacting with execution feedback, and repairing state-mutating operations. While agentic reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training small language models (SLMs), existing frame-works are predominantly tool-centric and struggle with data-centric environments. Specifically, applying tool-centric frameworks to stateful tasks like SQL debugging introduces severe spatial inefficiencies since proper environment isolation typi-cally requires a separate database container per rollout. Naively reusing databases without tracking evolving states across multi-turn interactions causes trajectory contamination and cross-trajectory interference, which severely destabilizes pol-icy updates. To overcome these bottlenecks, we introduce BIRD-RL , a novel four-stage agentic RL curriculum that alternately enhances foundational agent reasoning and trajectory-adaptive on-policy exploration to circumvent the insta-bility inherently associated with direct end-to-end learning. Crucially, to support this curriculum, BIRD-RL is driven by a Trajectory-scoped Persistent Execu-tion Infrastructure , which enables safe database replica reuse while preserving trajectory isolation. This design improves spatial RL efficiency by 12.8x , success-fully enabling scalable agentic RL for complex data-centric tasks without needs of Kubernettes. Empirical evaluations on the BIRD-CRITIC-SQLite benchmark demonstrate that our 7B and 14B models achieve Success Rates of 44.40% and 48.00%, respectively, showing competitive performance against strong proprietary model-based agents such as Claude-Opus-4.6 Agent (48.20%). Furthermore, BIRD-RL successfully trains unified SLMs capable of both advanced SQL debugging and generation, highlighting its broader potential for multi-objective optimization across heterogeneous, real-world data-centric tasks. For reproducibility, we release our code at https://github.com/bird-bench/BIRD-RL .

Driven by the rapid development of large language models (LLMs) [45, 60], a broad spectrum of LLM-based SQL tasks has emerged. Text-to-SQL, which translates natural language questions into executable SQL queries, has become a representative beneficiary of this progress [24, 57, 35, 23]. SQL issues remains a significant challenge with the evolving user demands in realistic scenarios [1, 30, 12, 51, 15]. Such SQL issue resolution often requires considerable manual effort, given the complexity of query semantics, intricate underlying schemas, and heterogeneous database operations. As a result, automating this process offers substantial practical value by improving efficiency and reducing reliance on specialized expertise [34, 62]. Automatic issue resolution inherently requires stronger agentic capabilities to conduct root cause location and repair through iterative interaction with the database environment [25, 54, 53]. Contemporary approaches to developing reliable debugging agents can be categorized into two primary paradigms: inference-time prompting [22, 53, 55] and post-training-based agent learning [25]. The former employs elaborate in-context orchestrations, in which LLMs iteratively refine intermediate reasoning decisions based on execution feedback. The latter paradigm strengthens small language models (SLMs) by internalizing these agentic capabilities directly into model parameters through supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Such agents are highly valuable in data-centric applications due to data privacy and security concerns [40]. This practical need motivates the focus of our work. Despite its effectiveness, SFT often fails to promote robust intermediate reasoning and generalizable repair behavior due to its heavy reliance on demonstrated correction trajectories [7, 13]. Agentic reinforcement learning (RL), instead, has recently emerged as a promising paradigm for training interactive agents, enabling models to improve reasoning through autonomous exploration driven by environmental reward signals [60, 3, 61]. However, agentic RL for SQL issue resolution presents unique, domain-specific challenges. On the one hand, SQL issue resolution requires a broader action space for root-cause analysis in declarative SQL. Agents often need to verify hypotheses through stateful database operations, including data manipulation ( INSERT , UPDATE , DELETE ) and schema modification ( ALTER TABLE ) [9, 19], placing a strong demand on the underlying infrastructure to support persistent and isolated database interactions at scale. General tool-centric frameworks often manage environments via a container-per-rollout mechanism [42, 54, 27]. However, applying this to data-intensive database environments incurs severe spatial inefficiencies and prohibitive scaling bottlenecks during massive trajectory generation. On the other hand, SQL debugging is a complicated task that simultaneously demands schema-grounded reasoning and dynamic environmental interaction. Direct end-to-end learning may overwhelm the policy within an exponentially large search space, resulting in suboptimal debugging effectiveness [10]. To bridge this gap, we introduce B IRD -RL, a novel agentic RL curriculum for SQL issue solving. It decomposes agent learning into four progressive stages: reasoning imitation (Section 3.1), tool-use imitation (Section 3.2), adaptive reasoning reinforcement (Section 3.3), and interactive agentic reinforcement (Section 3.4). Such a framework decouples an otherwise densely intertwined skill set into atomic, manageable learning objectives, circumventing the instability inherently associated with direct end-to-end learning. To realize scalable training for the final stage, we further introduce a 

Trajectory-Scoped Persistent Execution Infrastructure comprising a worker-partitioned database 2pool (Section 4.1) and a customized agent loop (Section 4.2), enabling isolated stateful interactions and high-concurrency efficiency via the orchestrated reuse of database replicas. Building on B IRD -RL, we release the B IRD -T ALON model family, including 7B and 14B versions. Furthermore, to demonstrate that our curriculum generalizes beyond single-objective optimization, we introduce B IRD -Z ENO -7B, a unified multi-task model trained under a principled data-mixing ratio for heterogeneous SQL tasks targeting both debugging and query generation. As a resource contribution to the community, we also release SIX-GYM-SQL ITE , an automatically constructed training environment that extends SIX-GYM [25] to the SQLite dialect to facilitate future research for SQL agents. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that B IRD -T ALON -7B and 14B achieve Success Rates (SR) of 

44.40% and 48.00% on BIRD-CRITIC-SQL ITE , respectively, performing competitively against strong proprietary model-based agents such as Claude-Opus-4.6 Agent (48.20%). Our trajectory-scoped infrastructure improves RL spatial efficiency by 12.8× under the evaluated SQLite workload, enabling stable and scalable agentic training. Besides, the unified B IRD -Z ENO model achieves an Ex-ecution Accuracy (EX) of 63.90% on the BIRD development set, delivering generation performance comparable to dedicated text-to-SQL models specifically optimized with RL while maintaining its debugging proficiency. These findings establish B IRD -RL as a scalable framework for data-centric agent training, with its trajectory-scoped infrastructure paving the way for agentic RL in heavy, stateful database environments, while underscoring the potential of multi-objective training for heterogeneous real-world SQL tasks. 

2 Preliminaries 

2.1 Task Definition Text-to-SQL Generation. Given a natural language question Q, with its corresponding database schema S, the goal of text-to-SQL is to generate the SQL query σpred that could answer the question 

Q accurately by: 

σpred = fθ (Q, S), (1) where fθ (·) refers to the mapping function with parameters θ applied by the generator. 

SQL Issue Resolution. Compared with SQL generation from scratch, SQL issue resolution is a more realistic and challenging task. The task takes as input a user-provided SQL query σissue , a natural language problem description P specifying the issue and intended behavior, and the database schema S. The goal is to produce a revised query σpred that resolves the issue while preserving the user intent by: 

σpred = fθ (P, S, σ issue ), (2) The target output σpred is expected to satisfy the underlying user intent inferred from the input triplet. 

2.2 Tool-Centric Agent RL Loop 

Given an SQL issue task x = ( P, S, σ issue ), a standard tool-centric agent loop [42] models interaction as a history-conditioned decision process. The agent maintains a textual history ht, initialized as 

h0 = x, and at each step samples a structured tool invocation at ∈ A from policy πθ :

at ∼ πθ (· | ht), ot ∼ fk(· | at, e t), ht+1 = ht ⊕ (at, o t), (3) where fk denotes the selected tool, ot is the returned observation under environment state et, and ⊕

denotes history concatenation. The trajectory ends when the agent submits a final answer or reaches the maximum step budget. This fundamentally tool-centric loop records tool feedback in the textual context while leaving the persistent environment external to the loop. For many agentic RL tasks [42, 54, 27], isolation can be approximated by assigning each rollout an independent sandbox. In data-centric tasks such as SQL issue resolution, however, this container-per-rollout design becomes prohibitively expensive, since each trajectory would require its own mutable replica of a potentially large database. Reusing database replicas is therefore necessary for scalability, but remains unsafe under a purely tool-centric loop. Without explicit trajectory-to-environment binding, tool calls from the same rollout 3may fail to access the intended evolving database state, while reused replicas may carry residual mutations from previous rollouts. Such state leakage contaminates execution feedback and induces cross-trajectory interference, thereby destabilizing on-policy RL. These limitations motivate the Trajectory-scoped Persistent Execution Infrastructure introduced in Section 4. 

3 BIRD -RL: Efficient Agentic RL Curriculum for SQL Agent Training 

Autonomous SQL issue resolution requires diagnostic reasoning, tool invocation, and feedback-guided refinement. Directly optimizing these capabilities end-to-end over a large interactive action space can lead to unstable or suboptimal policies [10]. To address this, B IRD -RL adopts a four-stage curriculum, as shown in Figure 1 (left), which contains Reasoning Imitation Section 3.1, Tool-Use Im-itation (Section 3.2), Reasoning Reinforcement (Section 3.3), and Interactive Agentic Reinforcement (Section 3.4). This progressive design decomposes agent learning from static reasoning to stateful interaction, while the final stage is supported by our trajectory-scoped infrastructure for scalable and contamination-free RL. 

3.1 Stage 1: Reasoning Imitation Training Objective. As the foundation of the curriculum, this initial stage aims to establish a strong reasoning prior for schema-grounded issue solving. Formulated as a single-turn sub-task, the target output for a given input task x = ( P, S, σ issue ) is defined as y = ( t, σ ), where t denotes the reasoning thought, and σ is the predicted query. The policy πθ is trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT). The structured diagnostic reasoning capability learned in Stage 1 effectively mitigates the naive exploration (e.g., SELECT * FROM table ) in the ultimate interactive scenarios, resulting in more efficient execution and preventing unnecessary context expansion. 

Training Data Construction. To reduce human annotation cost, we use Claude-Sonnet-4.5 

as the teacher model to construct reasoning-imitation data in the SIX-GYM-SQL ITE environment. Details of the environment construction and the protocol for preventing training-evaluation contamina-tion are provided in Appendix A. For each task x = ( P, S, σ issue ), the teacher generates a debugging thought t with access to the corrected query σ∗, which serves as a reference for constructing the reasoning that diagnoses the SQL errors and motivates the target repair. We pair the generated thought with the corrected query as the supervision target y = ( t, σ ∗). To ensure high-quality training data, we only include (t, σ ∗) pairs where the corrected SQL σ∗ can be regenerated from the debugging thought t and the regenerated solution passes all test cases in C. This produces 4,982 instances for the Reasoning Imitation stage. 

3.2 Stage 2: Multi-Turn Tool-Use Imitation Training Objective. Building on the previous stage, Stage 2 performs trajectory fine-tuning with the SQL-Act scaffold [25], where executable SQL statements serve as fine-grained actions for database inspection, hypothesis testing, and root-cause localization. Each trajectory τ = {(ti, a i, o i)}ni=1 

interleaves the agent thought ti, SQL action ai, and database observation oi. We implement SQL actions with two tools: execute_sql for exploration and testing, and submit_solution for termination. These tools keep SQL as the core action while providing an explicit stopping signal. Tool descriptions are detailed in Figure 6. 

Data Construction. Following the trajectory formulation defined above, the teacher model 

Claude-Sonnet-4.5 is guided to generate a verified trajectory ˜τ for each input task suite x. We further employ the f-plan boosting strategy [25] to augment successful candidate trajectories. We consider the query σ submitted via submit_solution as the predicted query of each trajectory and apply the identical verification protocol as Stage 1 to filter (x, ˜τ ) pairs. We ultimately collected a training set of 4,502 trajectories. 

3.3 Stage 3: Adaptive Reasoning Reinforcement RL Training Objective. Although the first two stages provide reasoning and tool-use priors, they are learned from offline imitation and may induce rigid patterns that fail to adapt to new interaction 4contexts, as evidenced in Section 5.5. To bridge imitation and on-policy agentic exploration, Stage 3 introduces a reasoning-focused RL phase that encourages adaptive reasoning before multi-turn RL. We adopt GRPO [41] for both reinforcement stages of B IRD -RL. In Stage 3, following the single-turn output format y = ( t, σ ), the policy samples a group of N candidate outputs {yi = ( ti, σ i)}Ni=1 for each input x. 3.4 Stage 4: Stateful Interactive Agentic Reinforcement Agentic RL Training Objective. As the culmination of the training curriculum, Stage 4 aims to enable autonomous multi-turn exploration with tool augmentation through agentic RL training. For each input task x, a group of N multi-turn trajectories {τi}Ni=1 is sampled. The final solution σi is determined by the submit_solution action when available, or otherwise by the last generated query. The optimization follows the GRPO objective in Eq. yi, and the reference policy πref is updated to the policy obtained from Stage 3. 

Reward Design. The objective of the final stage is to produce a functionally correct solution through iterative tool-augmented interaction. While prior works [37, 17] typically augment verifiable execution rewards with elaborately designed proxy rewards, we observe that these proxy rewards can induce reward hacking in SQL debugging, as demonstrated in Section 5.6. To this end, we adhere to the execution-based reward defined in Eq.(5) assigned exclusively to the final solution, providing an unbiased signal directly aligned with the stage goal. To encourage unconstrained exploration, we apply no process-level rewards. Eliminating the step-wise credit assignment prevents bias toward specific interactions and purely aligns optimization with the final functional objective. Detailed designs of the proxy-reward variants used for comparison are provided in Appendix B.2. 

4 Trajectory-Scoped Persistent Execution Infrastructure 

As discussed in Section 2.2, standard tool-centric loops record tool observations in the textual history but do not explicitly bind a rollout to a persistent execution environment. This design is insufficient for stateful SQL debugging, where each trajectory must retain the database effects of its earlier actions for subsequent hypothesis testing. To support scalable stateful RL with environment isolation, BIRD -RL introduces a Trajectory-scoped Persistent Execution Infrastructure, as shown in Figure 1 (right). It consists of two main modules: a worker-partitioned database pool (Section 4.1) for reusable storage and a customized stateful agent loop 4.2 for trajectory-level environment binding.

4.2 Customized Stateful Agent Loop 

Standard tool-centric loops [42] usually dispatch each tool invocation as a request-scoped call: the execution backend receives the current tool input and returns an observation, while the request identifier only represents this individual call rather than the entire rollout. Such request-scope abstraction fails to preserve state continuity within a trajectory, which is essential for SQL debugging with state-mutating operations. We therefore make the environment management trajectory-scoped with four designs: 1) Stable Trajectory Identifier Binding: at the start of each rollout, we assign a stable trajectory identifier, and all subsequent execute_sql calls within the same rollout use this identifier to retrieve the same database replica, ensuring intra-trajectory state persistence. 2) Lightweight Instance Registry: we maintain a lightweight registry that maps each trajectory identifier to its allocated database replica, e.g., (trajectory_id , worker_id , db_id ). This registry serves as the assignment memory between the agent loop and the database pool, routing each tool call to the intended persistent environment. 3) Deferred State Release: the assigned replica remains bound to the trajectory until termination, rather than being released after each tool call. It is normally released upon the terminal submit_solution action, while an asynchronous timeout mechanism reclaims replicas from abnormal rollouts to prevent resource leakage. 4) Dirty-Aware Lazy Reset: when a released replica has been modified, it is marked with need_reset=True and returned to the pool without resetting at trajectory termination. The reset is deferred until the replica is allocated to a new trajectory, which avoids termination-phase lock contention while ensuring that every new rollout starts from a clean database state. 5.3 Analysis of Database Reuse Efficiency. 

To validate our proposed pool-based container isolation strategy, we conduct an efficiency analysis of database lifecycle I/O under trajectory-level isolation. Unlike peak memory and storage capacity, which can be managed through resource provisioning and pool sizing, repeated database materializa-tion and restoration place sustained pressure on storage bandwidth. The resulting I/O saturation can cause rollout stalls or timeouts. Such infrastructure-induced failures can distort the reward signal and destabilize on-policy optimization if recorded as zero-reward trajectories. Reducing this recurring overhead is therefore important for feasible and reliable agentic RL over resource-intensive database environments. As shown in Table 3, the training-time I/O column presents the theoretical online I/O complexity during rollout execution. The related parameter definition and detailed derivation of the complexity terms is provided in Appendix D.2. The naive per-rollout baseline scales with the total number of generated trajectories. In contrast, the dynamic reusable pool fundamentally amortizes the database instantiation cost via cross-rollout reusability. This strategic shift leaves the online I/O dominated mainly by resetting modified replicas to preserve clean trajectory-level isolation. Benefiting from the SFT stages in our framework, which effectively regularizes the agent’s exploration behavior, only 

9.25% of the practical trajectories execute state-altering queries and actually trigger a database reset. As a result, our proposed method guarantees a superior I/O efficiency compared to the naive baseline. However, dynamic online allocation is vulnerable to abrupt memory spikes under imbalanced database workloads, which can cause capacity overflows and destabilize rollout execution. We thus apply a pre-allocated database pool to mitigate this risk. Under the evaluated SQLite setting with batch size B = 16 and rollout R = 16 , our design reduces the measured online lifecycle I/O by 12 .8×

compared to per-rollout containers. The trade-off is an offline pool cost of 69 GiB, which is practically negligible relative to the terabyte-level massive online I/O savings. Notably, since this offline pool scales strictly with the number of unique databases rather than the massive training volume N , its storage overhead remains predictable and highly cost-effective even under large-scale trajectory-level isolation. As the first work to investigate pool-based environment isolation for data-centric tasks, our infrastructure enables stable and scalable stateful agentic RL training for SQL debugging, paving the way for more intelligent database environment management as future work.

6 Related Work 

LLMs for Text-to-SQL. In recent years, the application of LLMs has improved the performance of text-to-SQL to another level of intelligence [59, 52, 38, 18]. Early research primarily leveraged the in-context learning capabilities of LLMs to build text-to-SQL systems through sophisticated prompt engineering [11, 35, 11, 39, 36]. Driven by the advent of language agents, recent text-to-SQL research has pivoted toward multi-agent architectures [47, 50, 6]. A critical component emerging from these architectures is the automatic error correction mechanism. Although recent agents increasingly incorporate automatic correction loops to improve response accuracy [43, 29, 36], the distinct challenge of debugging the full spectrum of SQL operations for real-world database administration remains largely unexplored. Our work is expressly designed to bridge this gap. 

RL for Reasoning. Reinforcement learning (RL) has recently emerged as a promising paradigm for improving the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, enabling models to autonomously learn effective policies with the guidance of reward signals [14, 32]. It has shown impressive success on reasoning-intensive tasks such as mathematical solving [41], code generation [26, 44], and visual reasoning [48, 46, 58]. In light of these advancements, recent research in the text-to-SQL domain has started to leverage RL-based frameworks to steer the query synthesis process [33, 56, 28, 8, 37, 17]. Our work aims to extend this line of research beyond static one-shot SQL synthesis to dynamic, stateful SQL issue resolution, where the agent must learn to diagnose and revise candidate repairs through multi-step interaction. 

7 Conclusion 

In this work, we introduce B IRD -RL, a four-stage agentic RL curriculum for SQL issue resolution. To enable scalable training in data-centric environments, we equip B IRD -RL with a Trajectory-scoped Persistent Execution Infrastructure that supports isolated stateful interactions through orchestrated database-replica reuse. Built on this framework, we release the B IRD -T ALON model family for SQL debugging. We further introduce B IRD -Z ENO , a unified multi-task model trained with a principled data-mixing strategy, to examine the generality of our curriculum beyond single-objective optimization. Together, these contributions establish B IRD -RL as a scalable framework for data-centric agent training, paving the way for agentic RL in heavy, stateful database environments and highlighting the potential of multi-objective training for real-world SQL applications. 10 8 Acknowledgments

Training LLMs as autonomous agents for complex tasks fundamentally requires an integrated environment that combines high-quality task suites, executable environments, and rigorous test verification. To efficiently support the massive trial-and-error exploration inherent in optimizing these agents via RL, with server-based database systems such as PostgreSQL and MySQL, the lightweight, in-process nature of SQLite eliminates the client-server overhead of engines like PostgreSQL, enabling the high-throughput parallel rollouts essential for large-scale SQL agent training. While the existing SQL debugging benchmark SWE-SQL has introduced SIX-GYM environment for PostgreSQL, it lacks a dedicated training environment specifically constructed for SQLite. To address this gap, we contribute SIX-GYM-SQLite as a practical resource extension of the prior work, employing it as the underlying sandbox to automatically synthesize the stage-specific training data for B IRD -RL. 

A.1 SIX-GYM-SQLite 

Following the SQL-Rewind methodology introduced in SWE-SQL, we construct SIX-GYM-SQLite by adapting the original SIX-GYM training environment to SQLite. The resulting task suite combines instances adapted from the original SIX-GYM task suites with additional instances synthesized from newly collected Stack Overflow posts. We retain 5,000 high-quality instances based on answer quality, SQL complexity, and taxonomic coverage. To prevent training–evaluation contamination, the SQLite adaptation preserves the database- and source-level separation established by the SWE-SQL construction protocol. Specifically, SIX-GYM-SQLite and BIRD-CRITIC-SQLite retain the database schemas of their respective counterparts in SWE-SQL, which uses disjoint database domains and instances for training and evaluation. At the source level, the inherited construction protocol excludes Stack Overflow posts used in BIRD-CRITIC from the training source pool by post ID prior to synthesis. During subsequent supervision generation for Stages 1 and 2, the teacher model is provided only with training instances and their reference solutions, without access to evaluation instances. Furthermore, we exhaustively compared all 500 ×5,000 train–test pairs using a dual-view TF-IDF audit over normalized issue descriptions and buggy SQL. A pair was flagged as a high-confidence near-duplicate only when the same training instance exceeded 0.85 text similarity and 0.90 SQL similarity. Only 3 of 500 tasks (0.6%) are highly similar to training tasks, and all three are permission-privilege-related tasks, a category that is inherently shared, since privilege management (e.g., GRANT/REVOKE 

and access-control patterns) is core database knowledge that neither training nor evaluation can meaningfully exclude. Excluding these three flagged evaluation tasks leaves our main conclusions unchanged. D.2 Comparison of Training-Time Database Lifecycle I/O 

Table 3 formalizes the database lifecycle I/O overhead required to maintain trajectory-level isolation during interactive rollout execution. The naive per-rollout container baseline instantiates a fresh database for every generated trajectory. Consequently, this approach incurs an I/O cost proportional to the total number of rollout trajectories, resulting in the I/O complexity of O(N R ¯S), where N is the number of training tasks, R is the number of rollout trajectories per task, and ¯S is the average database size. To circumvent this exhaustive overhead, we propose a dynamic reusable database pool that funda-mentally alters the database lifecycle for data-centric tasks. During interactive rollout execution, the pool continuously tracks the operation state, including the availability and modification status, of each database replica. A trajectory first tries to acquire an available clean replica for its target database. Under an ideal dynamic allocation scheme, a new replica is instantiated online strictly when the concurrent demand exceeds the capacity of existing available replicas. Upon trajectory completion, the leased replica is immediately released back to the pool, avoiding any naive re-instantiation. Crucially, to handle state-changing queries, the pool employs a dirty-aware lazy reset policy. To formalize the theoretical I/O reduction over the baseline, we decompose the dynamic pool’s training-time overhead into two analytical components: 1) Bounded Instantiation Overhead :Assuming database requests follow a uniform access distribution across unique databases within a training batch of size B, the dynamic pool size is upper-bounded by the maximum concurrency, 

B × R, limiting the replica creation I/O to O(BR ¯S). In practical large-scale RL settings (e.g., optimizing a 14B parameter model), GPU memory constraints dictate a small, fixed batch size (typically B = 16 ). Consequently, this guarantees that B ≪ N . 2) Regularized Reset Overhead :While the dynamic pool eliminates redundant materialization, it unavoidably introduces state-reset operations for modified replicas. We quantify such overhead using an example-effective count 

q = Q/R , where Q denotes the total number of rollout trajectories that actually trigger the reset action during training across all N R rollouts. Let k ≥ 1 denote the empirical I/O reduction factor of an in-place reset relative to full copy-delete materialization. In database systems, targeted state restoration (e.g., table-level truncation or snapshot rollbacks) is considered cheaper than a full physical instantiation. Consequently, the specific value of k depends on the underlying reset optimization deployed. The reset complexity is thus bounded by O(qR ¯S/k ). Notably, q is intrinsically manageable. The preceding Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) phase of our framework effectively regularizes the agent’s exploration behavior, preventing arbitrary database mutations. Empirically, only about 9.25% of the practical rollouts execute state-altering exploitation in our experiment. Combining both components, the total lifecycle overhead of the dynamic pool evaluates to O(( B + q/k )R ¯S), where B + q/k ≪ N . This establishes that the dynamic pool mechanism achieves vastly superior spatial and I/O efficiency compared to the naive per-rollout baseline. While the bounded creation overhead holds under a steady-state assumption, real-world RL explo-ration frequently encounters a highly imbalanced distribution. Concurrent rollouts can heavily skew towards hot databases, triggering sudden capacity overflows and allocation bottlenecks that destabilize the training pipeline. To systematically amortize this risk upfront, our actual implementation replaces dynamic online creation with a pre-materialized database pool. By shifting the database instantiation entirely offline, we decouple resource allocation from the rollout path, eliminating the risk of memory thrashing and I/O contention. Consequently, as rollouts acquire replicas via constant-time ( O(1) )pool lookups, the online training I/O is dominated by the predictable reset operations with complexity 

O(qR ¯S/k ).In our practical setting ( B = 16 , R = 16 ), we pre-reserve BR/ 2 replicas per database in the pool. Empirical observations confirm that this capacity is sufficient to avoid pool exhaustion. This comes with a one-time pre-materialized storage cost of 69 GiB as a trade-off, which is excluded from the online I/O reported in the table. Such a trade-off is practically negligible relative to the terabyte-level massive online I/O savings. E Limitations and Future Work 

This work only focuses on SQL tasks as a representative class of stateful data-centric environments. A natural future direction is to extend BIRD-RL to broader high-value data science tasks, such as data cleaning, transformation, workflow construction, and analytical decision-making, where agents must similarly reason over persistent states, execution feedback, even on top of large data. In addition, BIRD-RL currently adopts execution-based rewards to ensure faithful alignment with functional correctness. Future work may explore hybrid reward designs that combine execution signals with semantics-aware verification, while explicitly addressing reward hacking to preserve reliable optimization behavior in complex data-centric settings. This can further advance upper bound of our framework. 

F Ethics Statement 

In this work, although our method involves data synthesis, all data are derived from databases and resources that are publicly available, ensuring transparency and reproducibility. We take care to avoid sensitive or personally identifiable information, and the generated outputs are SQL queries rather than open-ended natural language, reducing the risk of biased or harmful textual content. 

G Broader Impact 

This work aims to advance scalable agentic RL for data-centric environments, where agents interact with live, stateful databases rather than static textual contexts. The proposed trajectory-scoped infrastructure may benefit future research on database agents by reducing the cost of isolated rollout execution and making RL training more practical for heavy database workloads. In the long term, such techniques could help build more capable SQL assistants that reduce manual debugging effort, improve developer productivity, and support privacy-sensitive applications through stronger open-source models that can be deployed locally. At the same time, beyond improving SQL issue resolution, our method points toward a future paradigm of general-purpose SQL agents. By training over heterogeneous SQL tasks, B IRD -RL 23 makes it possible to move beyond narrowly specialized models toward unified agents that can perform query generation, issue diagnosis, execution-based verification, and interactive repair within a single framework. Such agents could serve as a more general interface for real-world database applications, where users often need end-to-end assistance across multiple stages of database interaction rather than isolated text-to-SQL generation.
